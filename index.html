Why go solar

Your roof could be earning its keep.

Electricity you generate yourself is electricity you don’t have to buy. Here’s what that means in practice.

01

Lower electricity bills

Every unit your panels produce and you use at home is one less bought from your supplier — for the life of the system.

02

Get paid for what you export

Under the Smart Export Guarantee, energy suppliers pay you for surplus electricity you send back to the grid.

03

Protection from price rises

The more of your own power you use, the less exposed you are the next time energy prices climb.

04

A smaller carbon footprint

Solar generates electricity with no emissions at the point of use — quietly, with no moving parts.

FAQs

Questions people ask us first.

Something else on your mind? Call 07525 743786 and ask.

Do solar panels work on cloudy days?

Yes. Panels generate from daylight, not just direct sunshine. Output is lower when it’s overcast and in winter, and higher on long, bright days — which is why estimates are worked out across a whole year.

Do I need a battery?

No, panels work without one. A battery lets you store surplus daytime generation and use it in the evening, so you buy less from the grid. Whether it’s worthwhile depends on when you use electricity; we’ll show you figures both ways.

Do I need planning permission?

Most home installations in the UK fall under permitted development, so they don’t. Exceptions can include listed buildings, conservation areas and some flat roofs. We check this as part of your survey.

What is the Smart Export Guarantee?

It’s a UK scheme under which licensed energy suppliers pay you for electricity you export to the grid. Rates vary between suppliers and tariffs, so it’s worth comparing them once your system is installed.

How much maintenance do panels need?

Very little. There are no moving parts, and rain keeps most roofs clean enough. Your monitoring app shows generation, so any drop in performance is easy to spot.

Is my roof suitable?

South-facing roofs generate the most, but east- and west-facing roofs work well too. Shading, roof condition and available space all matter — the free survey gives you a straight answer.
